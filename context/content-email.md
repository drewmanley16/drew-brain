# Email Replies

Voice and structure rules for Drew's email, pulled from real sent mail
(brand outreach replies, fan/advice replies, quick logistics, declines).

## How this differs from IG/LinkedIn
Email is the most properly punctuated of all three — full sentences, normal
capitalization, no lowercase-casual style. Still warm and direct, never
corporate-stiff. Always signed off, never trails without a close.

## Universal shape
1. **Greeting** — "Hi [Name]," or "Hey [Name]," — Hey for people Drew has
   rapport with, Hi for more formal/first-contact.
2. **Thanks/acknowledgment** — "Thanks for reaching out!" / "Thanks for the
   quick response!" almost always opens the reply body.
3. **The actual content** — direct, short paragraphs (2-4 sentences each).
   No hedging, no padding.
4. **Sign-off** — "Best,\nDrew" by default. "Best regards, Drew Manley"
   (full name) only for more formal/distant contexts like a polite decline
   acknowledgment. Occasional emoji sign-off for casual back-and-forth with
   people he already has rapport with ("Yes sir 🫡").

## Voice rules
- Double exclamation points are normal for genuine enthusiasm ("Thanks for
  reaching out!!", "Have a great weekend!!") — not performative, used when
  actually excited/grateful.
- State asks and terms directly and confidently — no hedging when quoting a
  rate or scope ("my rate would be $300" not "I was thinking maybe around
  $300 if that works").
- Always leave a door open when there's future potential ("I'd also
  definitely be interested in exploring a longer-term partnership if the
  first collaboration goes well").
- For advice/fan replies: answer their actual questions directly, share a
  real personal example, don't lecture. End with encouragement, not a
  generic "good luck."
- Never corporate filler ("per my last email," "circling back," "as
  discussed"). Keep it human.

## Situations and how each plays out

### Cold brand pitch (outreach, not a reply)
Short. 3 short paragraphs: who Drew is + the stat that proves reach
("recently crossed 2,500 followers... strong reach with CS students"), why
this brand specifically fits his audience, then a low-pressure offer
("happy to send over recent content and analytics if helpful!"). Never a
hard ask — always framed as exploring, not requesting.

### Brand negotiation reply
Confirm the scope/fit first, then state the rate plainly as its own short
paragraph, then leave the door open for follow-up or long-term work.

### Fan / advice reply
Warmest tone of any email type. Validate their feeling first ("It's totally
normal to feel that way, so don't be too hard on yourself"), answer with a
real personal example (not generic advice), end with direct encouragement
("Keep grinding and let me know if you have any other questions!").

### Quick logistics / reactive reply
Very short — 1-2 sentences. "Sweet, thanks! Posting now" / "Yes sir 🫡" /
scheduling confirmations. No need to pad these out.

### Polite decline acknowledgment
Brief, gracious, doesn't over-explain or fish for a reconsideration. "Thank
you for the update. I understand the decision and appreciate your time
reviewing my profile." Slightly more formal sign-off here ("Best regards,
Drew Manley").

## Examples

### Fan/advice reply (full)
> Hi Simran!!
>
> Thanks for reaching out, it's super cool to receive these messages. It's
> totally normal to feel that way, so don't be too hard on yourself. I get
> imposter syndrome all the time in every aspect of my life: CS, content
> creation, gym, etc.
>
> I'd say the best way I avoid burnout is by working on things that are
> actually interesting to me. don't worry about being "perfect" right away...
>
> Keep grinding and let me know if you have any other questions!
>
> Best,
> Drew

### Brand negotiation reply (full)
> Hi Andy,
>
> Thanks for sending over the details! The scope sounds great, and I think
> PracHub would fit really naturally with my content and audience.
>
> For the dedicated collaboration Reel with the scope you outlined, my rate
> would be $300. I'm happy to discuss and find something that works for
> both of us.
>
> I'd also definitely be interested in exploring a longer-term partnership
> if the first collaboration goes well.
>
> Looking forward to hearing your thoughts!
>
> Best,
> Drew

### Cold pitch (full)
> Hey Michael,
>
> I'm a CS student and creator behind @drewisliving on insta, where I make
> content around AI, coding, recruiting, and software engineering. I
> recently crossed 2,500 followers and have been getting strong reach with
> CS students and early-career engineers.
>
> A lot of my content is specifically around internship recruiting and
> helping students find and apply to roles, so Simplify feels like a really
> strong fit for my audience. I'd love to explore creating content together
> or getting involved with any creator or ambassador initiatives you have.
>
> Happy to send over some recent content and analytics if helpful!
>
> Best,
> Drew
