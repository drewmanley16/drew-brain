# Instagram Reel Scripts

Voice and structure rules for @drewisliving scripts, reverse-engineered from
real drafts. Use with `skills/write-instagram-script.md`.

## Structure

Every script follows roughly this shape:

1. **Hook** (1 line) — direct callout or bold claim, no warmup.
2. **Credibility / stakes** (1-2 lines) — why this matters right now, or why
   Drew is the one saying it.
3. **Payoff** — the actual list, resource, or explanation. Usually numbered
   or named items (repo names, video titles, company names).
4. **Urgency line** — a reason to act now, not later.
5. **CTA** — always the same shape: "comment [WORD] and I'll send you the
   [link/repo/resource]."

Series content ("part 1 of...") ends with "follow for part 2" instead of a
comment CTA.

## Voice rules

- Lowercase by default. Don't force-capitalize sentence starts.
- No em dashes. Use line breaks or "and" instead.
- Minimal punctuation — periods optional, commas light.
- Texting abbreviations are fine: u, ur, im, tryna, asjed-tier typos are fine
  uncorrected in drafts.
- One thought per line. This is a spoken script, not an essay — pace it like
  someone talking, not writing.
- Self-deprecating or specific personal credibility beats generic authority.
  ("mid dude with a 3.4 gpa in high school who landed an offer" not "as
  someone experienced in recruiting")
- Name real, specific things: exact repo names, exact company names, exact
  video titles/creators. Specificity is what makes it feel researched instead
  of generic advice.
- No hashtags, no emoji.
- Open loops work: tease what's coming ("I'll show you the exact link in a
  second") before delivering it.

## CTA pattern (always use one of these)

- "comment [WORD] and I'll send you the [thing]"
- "follow for part 2" (series only)
- "follow for more [topic] tips" (standalone value posts)

Pick a CTA word that's one word, easy to type, on-topic (APPLY, GIT, RESUME,
CLAUDE).

## Examples

### Example 1 — resource drop / credibility hook
> The reason you are failing your OA's is because you havent used this repo
>
> Its called tech oa by fast prep
>
> And they have all the OA's from top companies like amazon, google, OpenAI
>
> ...
>
> So if you want the link to this repo follow + comment git and ill send it to you

Why it works: hook is a direct accusation ("you are failing because..."),
immediately names the fix, backs it with specific proof (company names,
"auto-updates"), single clear CTA.

### Example 2 — numbered list format
> 10/10 GH repos that will get you hired
>
> Techiecamps/devops-projects
> devops-projects - a massive list of real devops projects from beginning to
> advanced with full AWS scripts
>
> ...

Why it works: each item gets exact repo path + one-line plain-English
translation of what it is. No filler between items.

### Example 3 — urgency/news hook, multiple drafts of same idea
> If you're looking for a Summer 2027 internship, today might be the most
> important day of the recruiting season.
>
> Microsoft, TikTok, ByteDance, Capital One, Amex, and dozens of other
> companies just opened applications.

Why it works: Drew drafts 2-3 variations of the same hook to pick the
tightest one (see raw notes) — the winning pattern is: name the stakes →
name the companies → name the urgency reason → CTA. Don't overwrite the
hook with adjectives, let the company names carry the weight.

### Example 4 — series / personal story
> People underestimate how far landing a freshman year internship can take
> you in college
>
> So this is part 1 of what id do to land an internship if I was a freshman
>
> And this is coming from a mid dude with a 3.4 gpa in high school who
> landed an offer after his freshman year

Why it works: relatable self-deprecating credibility instead of a
"expert" framing. Ends on a forward tease + follow CTA, not a comment CTA,
because it's a series.
