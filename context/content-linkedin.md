# LinkedIn Posts

Voice and structure rules for Drew's LinkedIn, reverse-engineered from real
posts. Use with `skills/write-linkedin-post.md`.

## How this differs from Instagram
Same casual voice, but LinkedIn posts are grounded in a specific personal
story/moment rather than pure teaching or resource-dropping. IG scripts
teach something; LinkedIn posts happened to Drew and then get generalized
into a lesson. LinkedIn also allows full sentences/punctuation and
occasional bullet lists — it's not teleprompter-paced like IG.

## Structure

1. **Scene-setting hook** — state something concrete that just happened, in
   plain terms ("this week I had my final presentation for my internship at
   PayPal", "last august I was getting almost no responses from my
   internship apps").
2. **Specific detail** — real numbers, real stakes, real names. This is what
   makes it feel like a genuine update, not a LinkedIn platitude post
   ("15 minutes", "200 cold DMs", "1.2m views", "$80 beef rib").
3. **The turn** — explicitly bridge from the story to a lesson. Drew often
   flags this transition directly: "long story short:", "the takeaway here
   is", "so basically". Don't bury the lesson in subtext — say it.
4. **Generalize the lesson** — extend it past the specific story into advice
   that applies to the reader ("technical skills matter but you need to
   improve your communication skills more than anything").
5. **Optional bullets** — used for lists of steps, insights, or questions
   people asked. Short fragments, no punctuation needed.
6. **Optional sidebar/shoutout** — a genuinely unrelated, humanizing aside
   tacked on (a restaurant, a friend, a joke about the city). This keeps
   posts from reading like content marketing.
7. **CTA (optional, low-pressure)** — link to something (site, IG, previous
   post), invite people to reach out/comment, or nothing at all. Never a
   hard sell.

## Common misses to avoid
Drafts have drifted corporate when not anchored tightly enough to the real
examples. Specifically avoid:
- "out of my hands now" / any resigned-sounding corporate phrasing
- "genuinely made this internship what it was" / generic gratitude language
- forcing a big dramatic reflection when the real material is more casual
  (not every post is a lesson post — check example 4)
- smoothing out real phrasing into full formal sentences when the source
  material was more clipped/casual

## Voice rules

- Full sentences and normal punctuation are fine here (unlike IG) — but
  still casual, still no corporate language, still no em dashes.
- Lowercase is used inconsistently/casually, not as a strict rule like IG.
- Concrete numbers and named people/places > vague description.
- State the lesson explicitly, then generalize it — don't make the reader
  infer the point.
- Self-deprecating and honest framing over humblebrag ("I do actually think
  I was super lucky" not "hard work pays off").
- Sidebars/shoutouts are welcome and make posts feel human — don't cut them
  as "off topic."
- Occasional emoji is fine (sparingly, e.g. 🫡), not required.
- Avoid: "I'm incredibly grateful," "this journey taught me," "in today's
  landscape," generic inspirational wrap-ups.

## CTA patterns (pick one or none)

- Link to a personal site/project + invite feedback
- "follow @drewisliving on Instagram" for more of X
- Invite DMs/comments on a specific topic ("anyone is welcome to reach out")
- No CTA — just end on the lesson or a joke

## Examples

### Example 1 — reflection + explicit lesson + shoutout
> This week I had my final presentation for my summer internship at PayPal
> ...
> Long story short: you can spend the entire summer building a sick tool,
> but if you can't clearly explain what you did, why you did it, and the
> impact it has on your org, the people making the decision will never
> fully understand the value of your work.
> ...
> Shoutout Noah Schoenbaum this sushi was fire but not as fire as your kit

Why it works: real stakes (return offer decision), explicit lesson flagged
with "long story short," generalizes to a broader truth about AI-era
communication skills, then a completely unrelated human shoutout at the end.

### Example 2 — vulnerability + numbers + growth update
> 3 weeks ago I finally decided to stop being insecure and start posting
> content on Instagram. Now I'm about to cross 1.2m views. The hardest part
> about doing this kind of thing is starting - the rest is easy.

Why it works: names the fear directly (insecurity) instead of skipping to
the win, backs it with a real number, one-line takeaway, soft CTA to follow.

### Example 3 — story with bullet list + honest attribution
> I sent over 200 cold DMs and hit LinkedIns weekly connection limit + got
> ghosted by pretty much everyone
>
> But about a month after I messaged someone at Zendesk, he replied saying
> there might be an opening. Within three weeks after he followed up, I:
> - interviewed
> - accepted an offer
> - flew out to Berlin to onboard half way through my fall quarter
>
> I do actually think I was super lucky but the takeaway here is if you
> really want something you should be putting yourself in situations to get
> lucky

Why it works: doesn't dress up the failure rate (200 DMs, mostly ghosted),
bullets compress the fast outcome, explicitly credits luck instead of
overclaiming skill, then reframes luck as something you can engineer.

### Example 4 — casual life update, low stakes, no big lesson
> My first week at PayPal as an intern is over.
> Some very cool insights:
> - the more snacks/free food the office has the more people show up
> - austin is super underrated as a city...
> - texas barbecue is to die for but a single beef rib will run you $80
> - your boss will make or break your internship (and i got extremely lucky
>   with mine)
>
> also ima post more so get used to me on your feed and tell all ur friends

Why it works: not every post needs a big career lesson — casual "week in
review" posts with specific, funny observations work too. Ends with a
purely personal, low-effort CTA.
